I lead the **Tianyi Lab**, where we study how machines can learn the way strong learners do in an open world: choosing *what* to learn and *when* (curricula, data selection, test-time compute, epistemic exploration, self-evolving), looping specialists into generalists and evolving specialists from reusable generalists (routing, mixture-of-experts, agentic learning, collaborative & federated learning), and staying aligned with the world (neuro-symbolic world modeling) and with humans (interactive personal agents, cognitive alignment with humans). Our current work spans **LLM reasoning, multimodal and unified models, agents, and human–AI–world alignment**, with applications in education, science, design, visualization and embodied intelligence.

Recent recognition includes the **ACL 2026 SAC Highlight Award**, the **2026 Best Research Award** from the International e-Assessment Association, a **Best Paper Honorable Mention** at the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on AI-Native Academia and the **ICDM 2013 Best Student Paper Award**; I have been listed among Stanford's World's Top 2% Scientists since 2024. I serve as an Area Chair for NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL, EMNLP, AAAI and IJCAI.

I have advised 14 Ph.D. students at UMD, including a **2026 Apple Scholar in AI/ML** (Ming Li) and a **2025 UMD Flagship Fellow** (Dongping Chen). The [group](/group/) page has the full list and a note for prospective students.

#### Research themes

- **Human–AI–World alignment** — cognitive and epistemic alignment, human modeling, multi-objective alignment; neuro-symbolic and physics/geometry-grounded world models; interactive agents.
- **Self-evolving AI and sustainable learning** — data/task/environment selection, synthesis and curricula; continual learning; auto-benchmarking and auditing; self-reward and self-labeling.
- **Generalist–specialist cycle** — dynamic models, mixture-of-experts, federated and collaborative learning, multi-agent orchestration.
- **Interpretability and foundations of ML** — memorization, generalization, grokking, collapse, representation, hallucination and steering.

Papers by topic are on the [research](/research/) page; the complete list by year is under [publications](/publications/).
